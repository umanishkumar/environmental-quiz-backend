## Climate Change
Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns, driven largely since the 1800s by human activities, especially burning fossil fuels. Global average temperature has risen roughly 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels. Effects include more frequent extreme weather, rising sea levels, and shifting ecosystems. The Paris Agreement (2015) set a goal of limiting warming to well below 2°C, preferably 1.5°C, above pre-industrial levels.

## Global Warming
Global warming specifically refers to the long-term rise in Earth's average surface temperature due to increased concentrations of greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide and methane. It is the primary driver of broader climate change. The greenhouse effect traps heat in the atmosphere; without it Earth would be too cold to support life as we know it, but excess greenhouse gases from human activity amplify the effect beyond natural balance.

## Renewable Energy
Renewable energy comes from naturally replenishing sources: solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, and biomass. Unlike fossil fuels, these sources produce little to no greenhouse gas emissions during operation. Solar and wind capacity have grown rapidly worldwide due to falling costs. Renewable energy is central to decarbonizing electricity grids and reducing dependence on finite fossil fuel reserves.

## Biodiversity
Biodiversity is the variety of life on Earth, including genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity. It underpins ecosystem services like pollination, water purification, and soil fertility. Habitat loss, climate change, pollution, and overexploitation are driving what many scientists describe as a sixth mass extinction event, with species disappearing far faster than historical background rates.

## Ecosystems
An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with each other and their physical environment. Ecosystems can range from a small pond to an entire rainforest or ocean. They provide essential services such as nutrient cycling, climate regulation, and food production. Disruption to one part of an ecosystem, such as removing a keystone species, can cascade through the entire food web.

## Pollution
Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or products into the environment, affecting air, water, and soil. Sources include industrial discharge, vehicle emissions, plastic waste, and agricultural runoff. Pollution harms human health, wildlife, and ecosystems, and is linked to millions of premature deaths globally each year through air and water contamination.

## Water Conservation
Water conservation involves using water efficiently to reduce unnecessary use and protect freshwater supplies for the future. Agriculture accounts for around 70% of global freshwater withdrawals. Techniques include drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting, fixing leaks, and water-efficient appliances. Freshwater scarcity already affects billions of people, and demand is projected to keep rising with population growth.

## Waste Management
Waste management covers the collection, transport, processing, and disposal of waste materials. The waste hierarchy prioritizes reduction and reuse over recycling, and recycling over landfill or incineration. Poor waste management leads to pollution, greenhouse gas emissions (notably methane from landfills), and health hazards, particularly in developing regions lacking formal collection systems.

## Deforestation
Deforestation is the large-scale clearing of forests, often for agriculture, logging, or urban development. It is a major driver of biodiversity loss and contributes roughly 10% of global greenhouse gas emissions, since trees store carbon that is released when cleared, often by burning. The Amazon, Congo Basin, and Southeast Asian rainforests are among the regions experiencing the most significant forest loss.

## Sustainable Development
Sustainable development means meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs, balancing economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection. The UN's 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted in 2015, provide a global framework covering poverty, clean energy, climate action, and more, with a target date of 2030.

## Air Pollution
Air pollution consists of harmful substances in the atmosphere, including particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10), nitrogen oxides, sulfur dioxide, and ozone. Major sources are vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, and burning fossil fuels or biomass. The World Health Organization links air pollution to respiratory and cardiovascular disease, and estimates it contributes to several million premature deaths worldwide each year.

## Ocean Pollution
Ocean pollution includes plastic debris, oil spills, agricultural runoff causing algal blooms, and industrial chemical discharge. An estimated 8 million tonnes of plastic enter the oceans annually, harming marine life through ingestion and entanglement. Ocean acidification, caused by seawater absorbing excess atmospheric CO2, also threatens coral reefs and shell-forming organisms.

## Wildlife Conservation
Wildlife conservation focuses on protecting animal and plant species and their habitats from extinction. Strategies include protected areas, anti-poaching enforcement, captive breeding programs, and international agreements like CITES, which regulates trade in endangered species. Habitat loss and illegal wildlife trade remain the leading threats to endangered species worldwide.

## Green Technology
Green technology, or clean technology, refers to innovations designed to reduce environmental impact, including electric vehicles, energy-efficient buildings, carbon capture systems, and smart grids. It spans energy, transportation, agriculture, and manufacturing, aiming to decouple economic activity from resource depletion and emissions.

## Environmental Health
Environmental health is the branch of public health concerned with how environmental factors, such as air and water quality, chemical exposure, and climate, affect human health. It addresses issues like lead exposure, air pollution-related respiratory illness, and heat-related illness from rising temperatures, emphasizing prevention through environmental policy.

## Carbon Footprint
A carbon footprint is the total greenhouse gas emissions, measured in CO2-equivalent, caused directly or indirectly by an individual, organization, product, or event. It includes emissions from energy use, transportation, food, and consumption. Reducing a carbon footprint typically involves cutting energy use, choosing renewable energy, reducing air travel, and eating a more plant-based diet.

## Recycling
Recycling converts waste materials like paper, glass, metal, and plastic into new products, reducing the need for raw material extraction and the volume of waste sent to landfill. Not all plastics are equally recyclable; contamination and inconsistent sorting significantly reduce actual recycling rates in many regions compared to what is collected.

## Sustainable Agriculture
Sustainable agriculture aims to produce food while maintaining soil health, conserving water, and minimizing chemical inputs and environmental harm. Practices include crop rotation, reduced tillage, integrated pest management, and agroforestry. It seeks to balance productivity needed to feed a growing population with long-term ecological resilience.

## Energy Conservation
Energy conservation means reducing energy consumption through more efficient use or behavioral change, distinct from renewable energy which changes the source of energy. Examples include improving building insulation, using efficient appliances, and reducing unnecessary electricity use. It is often the cheapest and fastest way to cut emissions since it requires no new infrastructure.

## Environmental Protection
Environmental protection encompasses laws, policies, and practices aimed at preserving natural resources and ecosystems from degradation. Landmark examples include the U.S. Clean Air Act and Clean Water Act, and international frameworks like the Montreal Protocol, which successfully phased out ozone-depleting substances and is often cited as one of the most effective environmental treaties in history.
